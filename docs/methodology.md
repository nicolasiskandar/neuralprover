# Research design and methodology

## Research question and hypotheses

Primary question: under equal model inference, Lean execution, and wall-clock budgets, does LoRA adaptation plus best-first search solve more held-out Lean theorems than the unadapted model with greedy decoding? Secondary question: do state deduplication and search priority improve efficiency?

H1: task fine-tuning improves held-out tactic prediction and verified solve rate. H2: bounded best-first search improves verified solve rate over greedy decoding at equal budgets. H3: safe deduplication reduces Lean work without reducing solve rate. These are hypotheses, not established conclusions.

## Baselines, measures, and controls

Planned comparisons: base+greedy, base+bounded search, LoRA+greedy, LoRA+best-first, LoRA+best-first+dedup. Hold model revision, theorem split, decoding samples, per-theorem generation allowance, Lean timeout, and wall-clock budget fixed. Record attempted/verified counts, pass@1 and pass@10 with the sampling protocol, solve-time distribution, tactic calls, expanded nodes, generated tokens, invalid/timeout rates, and hardware use. Use paired bootstrap or binomial confidence intervals for solve rates once sample sizes permit. Tactic exact match is a diagnostic only: alternative valid tactics lower exact match, while matching a tactic does not establish proof completion.

## Established methods and possible contribution

LeanDojo/ReProver establish theorem/goal extraction, tactic generation, and retrieval-augmented proving as prior art. LoRA is an established parameter-efficient adaptation technique. Best-first search and deduplication are also established algorithmic patterns. A defensible contribution would therefore be a careful, controlled study of a resource-aware search policy or state-prioritization method on a declared Lean benchmark—not the mere combination of these components. The current work has not established a novel improvement.

## Data and limitations

The checked-in pilot includes two local Lean core examples and one Mathlib-backed replay example (`Nat.div_right_comm`). Its source imports and active namespace/open/variable context are reconstructed without importing the target theorem, and the tactic passes Lean replay and kernel verification. The pilot is only an integration fixture. The parser supports simple one-tactic declarations and reports unsupported blocks. File-level hashing assigns the one Mathlib row to train; it cannot populate disjoint validation/test sets. Theorem/proof similarity audits beyond duplicate source declaration IDs remain outstanding.

The pinned Lean frontend has a native InfoTree exporter (`neuralproof data trace`) and a conservative normalizer (`neuralproof data normalize-trace`). The latter chooses source-backed tactic spans, groups wrapper/macro duplicates, and associates declaration-less nodes only when their source range lies inside a unique named proof. It constructs context from module imports and active scope directives and retains explicit provenance. On pinned `Mathlib/Data/Nat/Basic.lean`, tracing emitted 1,463 raw nodes; normalization kept 40 source tactic transitions and excluded the rest as wrappers/non-parser nodes or span duplicates. The original file elaborated successfully, but the reconstructed contexts have not all been replayed independently. Those examples are trajectory-supervision candidates, not standalone theorem proof certificates; the existing narrow replay extractor remains the verified-row path.

The resumable native extractor (`neuralproof data trace-corpus --files 200`) completed the first 200 sorted Mathlib paths at revision `56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679` (file-list SHA-256 `f487dddd67ed7022875985f479aabccb46307660ec4c3b7ae3f2c414a67a2e2c`). It produced 6,221 tactic transitions across 176 files: 4,977 train, 798 validation, and 446 test. The file-level split contains 139 train, 15 validation, and 22 test files among files with examples. No fully qualified source declaration name crossed splits, there were zero duplicate example IDs and broad imports, and 199/200 source files elaborated through the tracer. `Mathlib/Algebra/Category/ModuleCat/Monoidal/Adjunction.lean` failed because the frontend hit the file's heartbeat limit, followed by elaboration errors; it is recorded in `file_stats.jsonl` and `summary.json`. The split is file-level but confined to the first sorted 200 paths (mostly Algebra and Category), so it does not represent all Mathlib domains. Further, successful whole-file elaboration establishes that the original tactic trajectories occurred in a valid Lean environment; it does not establish that reconstructed contexts alone replay. Use the data for next-tactic prediction with this caveat; theorem-solving evaluation should use the independently replay-verified corpus or add a validation/replay stage first.

Two properties of the mined rows limit what they can supervise, and both are handled explicitly rather than silently. 246 rows (4.0 %) have a target that is not a tactic: the driver records the `by` of a declaration header, the `where` of a structure instance, a term brace, or a placeholder as a tactic node with a source span. Every such row carries a `Lean.Parser.Tactic.*` syntax kind, so the syntax kind does not identify them; they are excluded by the shared `is_tactic_target` rule, which leaves 5,975 rows split 4,789 / 773 / 413. And 1,050 rows record a focus-local or conversion goal state, which is not the proof-global goal list the live client reports; rows are stamped with their scope, and training and scoring use the proof scope only.

The first 200-file corpus draft used `import Mathlib` in every replay context. An audit found this could import the theorem being mined; that 93-row corpus was discarded and replaced. The current clean run uses source imports plus active namespace/open/variable/local directives and does not include the target declaration. On the same deterministic sorted prefix (file-list SHA-256 `f487dddd67ed7022875985f479aabccb46307660ec4c3b7ae3f2c414a67a2e2c`), it processed all 200 files and returned 25 verified records across 11 files: 22 train, 1 validation, and 2 test. Split file counts are 9, 1, and 1; there are no repeated source declaration IDs and the corpus audit found zero broad `import Mathlib` contexts. It saw 3,059 theorem/lemma headers, recognized 262 supported simple headers, and recorded 237 selected candidate failures (104 multi-tactic bodies, 76 replay failures, 32 goal-inspection failures, 25 final verification failures). Earlier declarations in the same source file are not reconstructed, so failures may be missing-context cases. These figures characterize the parser's supported subset, not Mathlib extraction coverage. It checks one-line `:= by` headers and one-line tactic bodies, not arbitrary Lean syntax. File-level source hashing reduces within-file leakage but related lemmas across files may still cross splits. Detailed per-file counts are in `data/mathlib-200/summary.json` and `file_stats.jsonl`; prompt-formatted training files are in `data/mathlib-200/processed/`.

## Experimental status

An oracle static candidate list containing the three Nat source tactics solved those three mined examples under width-three/20-node search: 3/3 verified, one node expanded and two tactic executions per theorem on average, about 10.54 seconds per theorem, Wilson 95% interval [0.438, 1.000]. Since the correct source tactics were supplied as candidates, this only validates extraction-to-search-to-kernel integration. The two examples from separate modules were individually replayed and kernel-verified during extraction but are not included in that search result. The earlier local core smoke run was 2/2 with interval [0.342, 1.000].

A CPU training run used the clean 22-row train split with the pinned SmolLM2-135M base (Python 3.11.17, PyTorch 2.6.0+cpu, Transformers 4.49.0, PEFT 0.14.0, seed 17, one epoch, 22 steps). Training loss was 4.4752; no validation metric was computed. On the same two held-out test rows with a 6-node/width-3 bounded search, the base model and LoRA adapter each verified 0/2 (Wilson 95% interval [0, 0.658]); pass@10 was not measured. The adapter expanded 1.5 nodes and made 4.5 tactic executions per problem on average, versus 1.0 node and 3.0 calls for the base. All attempted tactics were invalid (6 base, 8 LoRA total), and GPU metrics were unavailable. With only two test theorems this is a smoke check, not a model comparison supporting the research hypothesis. Outputs are under `experiments/clean-base-test/` and `experiments/clean-lora-test/`. The earlier five-row run used a superseded broad-import dataset and remains historical debugging only.

The InfoTree corpus was preprocessed with the shared context+goal prompt and trained for 100 steps on CPU (a full epoch was estimated near two hours and stopped before checkpointing). The 100-step checkpoint's training loss was 4.6356 after 142.4 seconds. `neuralproof evaluate-tactics` then compared greedy top-1 base vs LoRA prediction on the same deterministic 50 of 446 held-out test examples, limited to single-line source tactics. Both were 0/50 exact matches (Wilson 95% interval [0, 0.07135]). This negative pilot does not support the fine-tuning hypothesis. It does show that the current small adaptation budget and base model are not yet producing the source tactic labels. It is a tactic prediction metric, not a proof success metric; the normalized contexts have not been independently replayed.

That 0/50 was subsequently shown to be a harness artifact rather than a model result, and the distinction matters for how the number should be read. The prompt ended with `Proof state:\n\n<state>`, and the base model mirrored the blank line: on the pre-canonical prompt the first line of the completion was empty in 12 of 12 sampled completions, and the 100-step adapter, trained on that format, emitted a leading blank line in 6 of 6. The harness then took that first line as the tactic, so a model that said nothing was recorded as a model that answered wrongly. Canonicalizing the goal state before the prompt removed the blank line (0 of 12 empty), and an echo-aware completion boundary now skips the local context, the `case` header, and the prompt's own labels when looking for the tactic.

After both corrections the same held-out comparison is still 0/50, but it is a different measurement: of the 50 sampled completions, 22 base and 2 LoRA completions contained no tactic at all and are reported as `silent_predictions`, and the remaining ones were wrong tactics (the base model echoed proof state and identifiers; the adapter mostly emitted the word `proof`). The 100-step adapter is 2 % of one epoch and was trained on the superseded prompt format, so it is out of distribution under the canonical prompt; it is not evidence about a properly trained adapter.

Two further corrections change what the metric is measured over. The InfoTree driver records parser fragments as tactic nodes: 246 of the 6,221 rows have a target that is not a tactic — `by` (101), `where` (58), `{` (56), `‹_›` (23), `(` (4), `.` (2), `1` (1), `2` (1) — and no tactic can answer them. They are excluded from both training and scoring by one shared helper, which leaves 5,975 rows split 4,789 / 773 / 413. Separately, 1,050 mined rows record a focus-local or conversion goal state, which is not the proof-global state the live client reports; rows are stamped with their scope, and training and scoring are restricted to the proof scope only, so that the model is trained and measured on the distribution it is actually used on. The held-out set goes from 446 rows to 413 with a tactic target and then to 342 proof-scope rows; the metric reports both exclusion counts.

Finally, the training budget itself was faulty. `TrainingArguments` reads any non-negative `max_steps` as a hard limit and stops when `global_step >= max_steps`, so the previous default of `0` ended every unbounded run after its first step — a full-epoch run reported 1 step in 3.2 seconds and looked like it had finished. A step budget of `0` is now rejected and `-1` (the transformers default, meaning "as many steps as the epochs need") is the unbounded value. This is a harness defect that produced a plausible-looking result, and it is recorded here because a run that stops early is otherwise indistinguishable from a run that learned quickly.

A full-epoch LoRA on the corrected pipeline (3976 proof-scope training rows, 1 epoch, seed 17, base revision `4e53f736cbb20a9a0f56b4c4bf378d9f306ff915`) is the first measurement that is not a pilot artifact. On the full eligible held-out set — 342 of 446 test rows, after excluding 33 non-tactic targets and 71 focus-scope rows — greedy top-1 exact match is 0/342 (0.0 %, Wilson 95 % CI [0.0, 0.0111]) for the base model and 21/342 (6.14 %, Wilson 95 % CI [0.0405, 0.0920]) for the adapter. The base model produced no tactic at all on 157 of 342 rows; the adapter produced a tactic on every row. The adaptation therefore moves the metric, but 6.14 % exact match on single-line source tactics is small, the contexts have not been independently replayed, and exact match is not proof success. No theorem-solving claim follows from this number.

The 21 correct adapter predictions are all tactics with no lemma arguments: `simp` (14), `ext` (5), and `rfl` (2). Every `rw`, `apply`, `exact`, and `simpa` target is wrong, and the lemma analysis attributes most same-family errors to lemma selection rather than tactic-family selection. A lemma-name pre-filter can therefore remove hallucinated candidates from a search (62 of 342 adapter predictions are refused), but it cannot supply the missing lemma, so it is not expected to raise exact-match or solve rates by itself. The filter is opt-in and uses two allowlists: the pinned Mathlib name index and the identifiers in the current context/goal state. On the 342 held-out source targets, which Lean accepted in the original files, it has 0 false rejects after excluding term elaborators such as `reassoc_of%`; the same check rejects 62/342 adapter predictions and loses none of the 21 correct ones. This is a plumbing/measurement check, not evidence of improved proof solving, and the filter is never a substitute for kernel verification.

A retrieval-feasibility study tested whether Mathlib lemma retrieval could supply the missing arguments. A theorem-statement corpus (361,832 declarations) was built from the pinned environment, and deterministic BM25 indexes over lemma names and names plus pretty-printed types were evaluated on the 342 held-out targets. Only 84 rows (24.6%) have an in-corpus theorem reference after separating local and out-of-corpus references; on those rows, name + type recall at 1/5/10/20 is 4.8% / 28.6% / 32.1% / 34.5%, and excluding the target's own source module drops the top-20 rate to 9.5%. The aggregate is concentrated: 18 of the 29 hits at 20 come from one Mathlib module, and `apply`/`exact`/`refine`/`obtain` are at 0%. Lexical retrieval is therefore not adequate as a source of lemma arguments, and no retrieval was integrated into prompts, decoding, or training.

A follow-up compared a dense MiniLM index and a reciprocal-rank-fusion hybrid with BM25 on the same rows. The encoder (`sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, revision `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`, mean pooling, max length 128) was fixed before evaluation, as were RRF depth 50 and k=60. Dense / goal-target recall at 1/5/10/20 is 2.4% / 11.9% / 14.3% / 15.5%; hybrid is 3.6% / 21.4% / 32.1% / 36.9%. Hybrid gains two rows at 20 over BM25 (31 vs 29 hits) but loses early precision (@1 4.8% to 3.6%, @5 28.6% to 21.4%). With same-module exclusion, dense / hybrid / BM25 reach 6.0% / 13.1% / 9.5% at 20. The families that need lemma arguments (`apply`, `exact`, `refine`, `obtain`) remain at 0% for every retriever, and 19 of the 31 hybrid hits at 20 still come from one Mathlib module. Dense and hybrid retrieval are therefore also inadequate, so no retrieval was integrated into prompts, decoding, or training. A reranker cannot exceed the measured 36.9% top-20 ceiling on this sample; any future attempt needs a stronger temporal/duplicate split and must be judged by kernel-verified tactic acceptance rather than retrieval recall. Details and caveats: `experiments/retrieval-feasibility/README.md`.
